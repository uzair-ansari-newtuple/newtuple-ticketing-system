# DC Quality Control: Demo Script

**Audience:** a retailer's DC operations leader, QC manager, or inventory control lead.
**Length:** 10 minutes of demo, 5 minutes of questions.
**Device:** a phone or tablet for the floor flow, a laptop for the back-office views.

---

## The one-line pitch

> "Every unit that enters your DC is checked against the purchase order, with a photo and a count, and any problem reaches a supervisor before the stock reaches a store."

## The problem you open with (30 seconds)

Say it in the retailer's words, not ours:

- "Receiving is where inventory accuracy is won or lost."
- "Today the check is a paper sheet, a spreadsheet or someone's memory. Short and damaged shipments are found weeks later, after the vendor window to dispute has closed."
- "Supervisors learn about problems from a phone call, not from a system."

**Three outcomes the demo proves:** counts that match the PO or get flagged on the spot, photo proof for every UPC, and a supervisor queue with a full audit trail.

---

## Before you start (2 minutes of prep)

- One shipment ready in **Ready for QC** with 3 UPCs. Pick UPCs so that one will match, one will be short, and one will have two pallets.
- The phone or tablet signed in as a **QC associate**. The laptop signed in as a **supervisor**.
- Sample photos available, or a few real items on the table.
- Keep the **expected numbers** for each UPC on a card so you never do math live.

---

## The story: one shipment, start to finish

### Scene 1. Shipments arrive (1 min)

**Do:** Open the app, go to **Shipments**.
**Say:** "The purchase orders and shipments come straight from your system. The associate sees every shipment waiting for QC, with vendor, UPC count and units."
**Show:** The list, the status chips (Ready for QC, QC in progress, QC complete, Receipt closed).
**Point out:** "Nothing is typed in by hand. Pulling the list is one tap and it also happens when the screen opens."

### Scene 2. Start QC (30 seconds)

**Do:** Tick the shipment, tap **Start QC**.
**Say:** "One tap starts the check. The shipment now shows as in progress, and everyone can see it."

### Scene 3. Check the product (2 minutes)

**Do:** Pick the first UPC. You are on **Check the product**.
**Say:** "The associate opens one case and compares it with the order. Style, colour, UPC and price are all shown as expected values, so there is nothing to look up."
**Do:** Tick the five boxes. Take a photo (or tap a sample photo).
**Say:** "A photo is required. You cannot move on without proof. That photo is attached to the UPC for good."
**Point out:** The Next button stays disabled until all five boxes are ticked and a photo exists.

### Scene 4. Count the units (2 minutes) — the core moment

**Do:** Continue to **Count**. Enter units per bundle, bundles per case, full cases and loose units.
**Say:** "The app does the arithmetic, so no one needs a calculator. Cases times bundles times units, plus loose units."
**Show:** The progress card at the top. As you type, **Counted so far**, **Remaining** and **Total variance** move live, with the variance as a percentage.
**Do (match case):** Enter the exact expected numbers. The bar fills and says nothing remaining.
**Say:** "When the count matches, the associate just continues."

### Scene 5. Confirm the labels (1 minute)

**Do:** On **Confirm the labels**, take a photo of the pallet and case labels, tick both boxes.
**Say:** "Labels are confirmed with a photo, so what left the dock is traceable."
**Do:** Answer **Does this UPC have another pallet?** Say **No, finish this UPC**.
**Say:** "The UPC is approved. The shipment moves forward by itself when every UPC is done."

### Scene 6. When it does not match (2 minutes) — the money moment

**Do:** Open the second UPC and count a smaller number than expected.
**Show:** The red remaining count and the variance percentage.
**Say:** "This is where you normally lose money: the shipment is short and nobody notices until the invoice is paid."
**Do:** Tap **Report a problem**. The reason **Short shipment** is suggested. Add a note, send to supervisor.
**Say:** "The UPC is held. The associate cannot just finish it. A supervisor decides."
**Point out:** A UPC more than 10% away from the order cannot be finished by the associate. That limit is configurable.
**Do (optional, 30 seconds):** On the third UPC show **Yes, check the next pallet**. "Large UPCs often span several pallets. The app adds them up for you."

### Scene 7. The supervisor decides (1 minute)

**Do:** Switch to the laptop, signed in as supervisor. Open **Exceptions**.
**Say:** "The supervisor sees every held UPC with the reason, the numbers, the photos and the associate's note. They approve it, send it back for a recount, or mark it as not to be checked."
**Do:** Approve or send back one exception.
**Point out:** Every step leaves a comment on the record, with who and when.

### Scene 8. Close the shipment (30 seconds)

**Do:** Open the shipment. Submit QC, then verify receipt.
**Say:** "The system will not let a shipment close while any UPC is still pending or on hold. That rule is enforced by the workflow, not by trusting people to remember."
**Show:** Status moves to QC complete, then Receipt closed.

---

## The closing view: the manager's dashboard (1 minute)

**Do:** Open the **DC Quality Control** dashboard.
**Say:** "Everything the floor just did feeds this view: pass rate, rejected units, which suppliers cause the most problems, which categories fail most, and what needs attention today. This answers four questions: how much was inspected, how much passed, who is causing problems, and what is urgent."

---

## Five things to repeat

1. **Photo proof on every UPC.**
2. **The app does the math.**
3. **Mismatches are caught at the dock, not weeks later.**
4. **A supervisor decision, with a full audit trail.**
5. **The workflow enforces the rules** (no closing with open problems, no skipping photos).

## Questions you will get

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Does this connect to our ERP / WMS?" | "Purchase orders and shipments come in through a connector. Counts and results can go back the same way. The connection is configured per retailer." |
| "Can we change the rules?" | "Yes. The variance limit, the checklist items, the reasons and who approves are all settings of the workflow." |
| "What do associates need?" | "A phone or tablet with a browser and a camera. No installation." |
| "What about different roles?" | "Associates, supervisors and managers each get their own permissions." |
| "Can we see history?" | "Every step is recorded on the shipment with who, when and why." |
| "How long to roll out?" | "Pilot at one DC first, then expand. Tell us your PO source and we scope the connection." |

## Close (30 seconds)

> "In ten minutes you saw a shipment go from arrival to closed, with photos, counts that add themselves up, and problems that reach a supervisor straight away. The next step is a pilot at one DC with your own purchase orders and your own checklist. Which of your DCs would be the best place to start?"

---

### Presenter tips

- Do not explain screens, tell the story of the shipment.
- Keep the match case fast and spend your time on the mismatch.
- If a photo is slow, use a sample photo and keep talking.
- Never type numbers live without the expected-numbers card.
